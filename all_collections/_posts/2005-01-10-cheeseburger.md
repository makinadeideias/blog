![Cheeseburger](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makinadeideias/blog/main/assets/images/cheese.png){: style="float: left; width: 50%; margin-right: 20px; margin-bottom: 10px;" }Recently, I received an audio message from a colleague who offensively asked me in a provocative tone: "Why do you keep posting things in English? Do you think everyone speaks or can read English? We live in Brazil, yet you publish in English as if it were our native language. Not even your family can speak English…"

His words alerted me to something I find very important to declare to everyone who speaks and loves other languages: If you do not practice a language, you will lose it. You will lose fluency, you will lose vocabulary, and you will lose what you made all that massive effort to achieve. Language is dynamic, fluid, and, above all, alive. Language is meant to be used (all languages are). Language is meant for communication, for speaking, for connecting. I have never used my English to make fun of anyone or to brag about being better than anyone else.

Were that colleague aware of how much I have studied, read, and written, how much I have invested in different materials, how much I have spent on traveling, and how much time I have dedicated to learning the little I know, he would certainly say: "Use your English even more, bro, please do it."

An English teacher has to study a lot; we devote a tremendous amount of time to this craft. Not everyone can go to the States with daddy’s money or go on fancy and expensive exchange programs in Europe. While some people are hitting the clubs, most of us are sweating over grammar books or binge-watching TV series without subtitles. While there are people playing sports, we are poring over the Hemingways and the Hawthornes, analyzing poetry of the nineteenth century, battling with Longman dictionaries and Jane Austen novels, investing hours to unravel the mysteries of the language.

On top of that, I have to say that I was once an English student too, struggling to learn the basics. I also studied with the English File book series and wanted to master the odd TH pronunciation. I was just another learner thinking I would never understand the many mysterious uses of GET. Phrasal verbs and prepositions were also my particular hell. I spent years working on my pronunciation and listening skills. I listened to lots of Speak Up dialogues and read a plethora of Penguin Readers books to beef up my vocabulary.

We earn a degree, complete a specialization, take several extension courses—I’ve lost track of how many courses I have taken to learn about this or that. And that colleague still thinks I shouldn’t post in English? WTF? No donuts for you, bro!

Were my French at the same level as my English, I would post this text in French too, but I am still not that confident. I NEED AND LIKE TO SPEAK, TO COMMUNICATE IN ENGLISH! I am not showing off, but my English is much better than it used to be only because I have kept this fire burning, only because I stayed on duty—practicing as much as possible. If I could give a piece of advice to anyone reading this text, it would be this: Use your English, speak it, and practice it as much as possible.

Lucky for him Facebook doesn’t offer audio posts. If I could, I would speak English all day.

You can neither learn a language by spending a week at Disney nor through a miraculous three-month magic technique. There is no direct download for American English. I spent many years unsure of my linguistic skills, avoiding public speaking in English. Despite all that effort, my English is not native-like. Not even close. And I don’t want it to be. I have an accent! A strong version of a Brazilian English accent! A wonderful accent. I love it! It is entirely mine and was forged in my soul. Like it or not, deal with that and read Marcos Bagno!

If you want to learn or are learning a foreign language, use it to write posts on your social media. Venture out, practice! The only way to learn a language is to use it! Far be it from me to hate my native language. I love Portuguese, but I chose to be an English teacher, so I practice, I speak, I think, I dedicate myself, and I dream in this wonderful language.

So do not feel offended if my posts are in English. Facebook is social, language is social, we are social beings. In case you want to avoid me, you can block me, unfriend me, or even start learning the beautiful language this is all about. It is a pity that friend will never read this post. But I am not sorry!

I come from the ghetto and English is my crush.
I declare common ground with you, eager learner!
